What Is Crypto Staking?

Published on 22nd July, 2026

If you've spent some time learning about cryptocurrency, you may have come across the term crypto staking. It's often mentioned as a way to earn rewards on certain digital assets without actively trading them.



But what exactly is staking, how does it work, and what should beginners know before getting started? In this guide, we'll explain crypto staking in simple terms, explore how staking rewards work, and discuss the risks you should understand before staking your cryptocurrency.



What Is Crypto Staking?

Crypto staking is the process of committing certain cryptocurrencies to help support the operation and security of a blockchain network.



Many blockchains use a system called Proof of Stake (PoS) to help validate transactions and maintain the network. People who stake eligible cryptocurrency can help participate in this process, either directly or through a staking service.



In return, they may receive staking rewards. Think of it as participating in the operation of a blockchain network and potentially earning rewards for doing so.



However, not every cryptocurrency supports staking. Whether you can stake an asset depends on the blockchain and the services available for that asset.



How Does Crypto Staking Work?

To understand staking, it helps to know a little about how blockchains operate.



A blockchain is a digital record of transactions maintained across a network of computers. These computers work together to verify transactions and keep the record consistent.



On a Proof of Stake blockchain, validators help verify transactions and participate in adding new blocks to the blockchain.



Staking helps support this process. Here's a simplified explanation of how it works:

  • Choose a supported cryptocurrency: You need an asset that can be staked on its blockchain or through a supported staking service.

  • Stake your crypto: You commit your eligible assets through a supported wallet or staking platform. Navox is a very good example.

  • The network operates: Your stake may contribute to the network's security and transaction validation, depending on the staking method.

  • Rewards accumulate: Your rewards accumulate and your assets and profits will be paid into your wallet after the staking period is over.



What Are Crypto Staking Rewards?

Staking rewards are amounts of cryptocurrency distributed to eligible participants for taking part in a staking system.



The rewards may come from newly issued tokens, transaction fees, or other mechanisms defined by the blockchain or staking service. For example, suppose you stake 100 units of a cryptocurrency and the staking service advertises an annual reward rate of 5%.



If that rate remained constant for a full year, and you received the equivalent of 5% of your original stake in rewards, you would earn 5 units.



You would then have 105 units before accounting for fees, taxes, changes in the reward rate, or any losses.



This is only an illustration. Actual rewards can vary, and the market value of the cryptocurrency may rise or fall.



What Is APY in Crypto Staking?

APY stands for Annual Percentage Yield. It describes the potential annual return when the effect of compounding is included.



Compounding occurs when earned rewards are added to your balance and can themselves earn rewards.



For example, a staking service might advertise an APY of 4%. This means that your investment will grow by 4% in market value over a year.



Your actual outcome depends on several factors, including:

  • The amount of cryptocurrency you stake.

  • The reward rate and whether it changes.

  • Fees charged by the staking provider. Some platforms like Navox do not charge any fees for staking

  • Changes in the cryptocurrency's market price.



It's also important to distinguish APY from APR. APR generally represents an annual rate without accounting for compounding, while APY includes its effect.



Which Cryptocurrencies Can You Stake?

Staking is available for certain cryptocurrencies whose blockchains use Proof of Stake or a related mechanism.



Examples include:

  • Ethereum (ETH): Supports staking through its Proof of Stake network.

  • Solana (SOL): Supports staking through its validator system.

  • Ripple (XRP): Supports staking through its network's staking mechanism.

  • Cardano (ADA): Supports staking through its delegation system.

  • Polkadot (DOT): Supports staking through its network's staking mechanism.



These examples illustrate native staking opportunities, but the requirements and available services differ for each network.



What Are the Benefits of Crypto Staking?

Staking has several potential benefits for people who understand how it works.



  1. Potential to earn rewards

    Staking may allow eligible cryptocurrency holders to receive additional tokens without actively trading their assets.


  2. Supports blockchain networks

    On Proof of Stake networks, staking helps support the process used to validate transactions and maintain network security.


  3. Different participation options

    Depending on the blockchain, users may be able to stake directly, delegate to a validator, or use a supported service. Some options require less technical knowledge than others.


  4. Suitable for long term approach

    People who already intend to hold a supported cryptocurrency for the long term should consider staking as one way to participate in its network and earn passive income.



Final Thoughts

Crypto staking is a way to participate in certain blockchain networks while potentially earning rewards on eligible cryptocurrency.



The process may involve staking directly, delegating to a validator, or using a supported staking service. The available options and conditions depend on the cryptocurrency and provider.



Staking is one of the safest ways to earn passive income with your crypto assets especially for beginners.
